## Why This Space Is Not as Crowded as It Looks

When people first see I GET IT DONE, they assume we are a to-do app. We are not. When
they look closer, they might call us a calendar, a planner, a Pomodoro timer, or a
habit tracker. We are none of those things alone.

I GET IT DONE is an **ADHD task-initiation app.** And that distinction is everything.

The market is flooded with productivity tools — Todoist, Things, Notion, Apple Reminders,
TickTick, a thousand others. They all compete on the same axis: *who can hold the most
stuff?* More lists. More tags. More projects. More fields. More calendar views.

For a neurotypical user, "more" is a feature. For an ADHD brain, "more" is the enemy.
Every additional list, tag, and view is one more decision — and decision fatigue is the
exact thing that stops an ADHD user from ever starting.

To understand why we win, it helps to look at how the founder of Granola — a company that
beat Google, Zoom, and Microsoft in AI note-taking — explains what it takes to build a
product people actually love in a market full of existing tools.

In a recent interview, Granola founder Christopher Jelley introduced a simple but powerful
framework for evaluating whether a product can win. It is a 2x2 matrix built around two
questions:

1. **How frequently does the user need this?**
2. **How important is it to them when they do?**

---

## The Framework That Explains Our Advantage

|                              | **Low Importance to User**                                                                 | **High Importance to User**                                                                                          |
|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **High Frequency** (Daily/Weekly) | Used often, but not very important. Hard to win here — users treat it like a utility and won't switch for a slightly better version. | **The Winning Zone.** Used often *and* critical. Even a 10% better experience is enough to make someone switch and never look back. |
| **Low Frequency** (Rarely)   | Used rarely and not important. No real market here.                                         | Used rarely but critical. Hard to build habits; users default to general-purpose tools.                              |

> "You want to be in that corner where it's very important to people — where if the product
> experience is even just 10% better, that's reason enough for people to switch to you and
> use it."
> — Christopher Jelley, Founder of Granola

Here is the key insight: most to-do apps are stuck in the **top-left box.** People open
them often, but they don't *care* about them. A checklist is a checklist. Nobody loves
their Reminders app. Nobody brags about their Notion setup (they brag about *building* it,
then abandon it three weeks later). These tools are utilities, and competing on utility
alone is a race to the bottom.

I GET IT DONE lives in the **top-right box.** For a person with ADHD, getting the next
thing done isn't a convenience — it's the daily battle that determines whether they feel
capable or defeated. It is both **frequent** (every single day) and **deeply important**
(it's tied to their self-worth, their job, their relationships). Here is why we earn that
corner.

## How I GET IT DONE Earns the Top-Right Quadrant

Every feature we ship is tied to a moment that is both **frequent** and **emotionally
high-stakes** for the people we serve.

| Feature | How Often It Happens | Why It Matters Deeply |
|---------|----------------------|------------------------|
| **Quick Dump** (brain dump, typed or spoken) | Many times a day — every time a thought intrudes | ADHD working memory leaks. A thought not captured in 10 seconds is gone, and the anxiety of "I'm forgetting something" never stops. Capture *is* relief. |
| **The 4-Lane System** (Now / Soon / Later / Park) | Every time a task is sorted or reviewed | The #1 ADHD blocker is prioritizing. An infinite list is paralyzing. Four buckets turn an impossible decision into a one-tap choice. |
| **Break It Down** (subtasks) | Every time a task feels "too big to start" | Task paralysis is rarely about effort — it's about the vague, scary size of a task. Breaking it into doable chunks is the difference between starting and stalling. |
| **Focus Mode** (one task at a time) | Daily, whenever overwhelm hits | Seeing 30 tasks freezes an ADHD brain. Seeing *one* unlocks action. Removing choice is the feature. |
| **Focus Timer** (10 / 15 / 25-min sprints) | Multiple times a day during work | Time blindness is a core ADHD trait. A visible countdown makes time real and turns "I'll do it later" into "I'll do it for 15 minutes." |
| **Daily Dumps** (morning / midday / evening) | Three times, every single day | Consistency is the hardest thing for ADHD. Gentle, time-boxed rituals build the rhythm that medication and willpower alone never sustain. |
| **Streaks, XP & Confetti** | Every completion, every day | ADHD is a dopamine-deficit condition. The reward loop isn't a gimmick — it's the neurochemistry the brain is missing, delivered on purpose. |
| **Weekly Reset** | Once a week | Without reflection, stale tasks pile up into shame. A clean weekly review turns "I'm so behind" into "look what I actually did." |

When you look at it this way, I GET IT DONE is not competing with Todoist or Apple
Reminders. **We are competing with the moment an ADHD person sits down, feels the weight
of everything they're behind on, and freezes.** That freeze is the real competitor. And it
is one we can beat — because every other app *adds* to the weight, and we are the only one
designed to lift it.

## What Makes Us Different: The "Personal" Advantage

Granola's other key insight was this: large enterprise tools are built for *companies* to
track *employees*. They are not built for the individual. Granola won by being a personal
notepad — something that belonged to the user, not to their manager or their IT department.

I GET IT DONE operates from the same philosophy — with an even sharper edge.

Every productivity app on the market is built for the **neurotypical brain.** They are
designed by people whose working memory holds, whose attention sustains, whose motivation
shows up on schedule. Then they're sold to everyone — including the millions of people for
whom "just make a list and work through it" has never once worked.

I GET IT DONE is built for the **ADHD brain first.** Not as an afterthought. Not as an
"accessibility mode." From the ground up:

- We **reduce** decisions instead of offering infinite configuration.
- We **surface one thing** instead of showing everything.
- We **reward immediately** instead of assuming intrinsic motivation.
- We **never punish** — deleting a task never lowers your stats; a missed day doesn't
  instantly kill your streak (we protect it once).
- We **never block capture** — even free users can always dump a thought, because a tool
  that stops you from getting it out of your head has failed at its one job.

It is not a generic productivity tool that ADHD people can tolerate. It is an
ADHD person's tool that happens to be powerful enough for anyone who's ever felt
overwhelmed — which, increasingly, is everyone.

## Voice + AI: The Invisible Handrail

Granola's founder describes the best AI design as an **"invisible handrail."** A handrail
on a staircase is always there. You never notice it — until you trip. Then your hand shoots
out and it needs to be right there, strong enough to hold your weight. That is the goal: AI
that stays out of the way until the exact moment you need it.

Our voice capture is that invisible handrail.

The most dangerous moment for an ADHD user is the gap between *having a thought* and
*capturing it.* Typing is friction. Friction is where the thought dies. So when the moment
hits — in the car, mid-conversation, walking out of a meeting — the user just **talks.**

- They hold one button and say everything on their mind, in one messy stream:
  *"I need to call the dentist, email Sarah the invoice, pick up dish soap, and start the
  Q3 deck."*
- Our AI (powered by GPT-4o-mini) listens and does the work the ADHD brain can't do in
  that moment: it **separates one rambling sentence into four clean, individual tasks** and
  filters out the noise ("um, let me think…", "is this thing on?").
- The audio is transcribed, the tasks appear ready to sort, and the recording is
  **immediately deleted** — never stored.

The user never has to think about "the AI." They just speak, and their chaos comes back to
them as order. That's the handrail: invisible until the moment of need, then exactly strong
enough to catch them.

> **How it works in practice:** The user provides the messy human input. The AI provides
> the structure the ADHD brain struggles to impose in real time. The user stays in control —
> they still choose where every task goes. We don't replace their judgment. We remove the
> friction that was stopping them from acting on it.

## The Competitive Moat in Plain Language

Here is what the marketing team needs to internalize and communicate:

The problem is **not** that people lack a to-do app. Everyone has one. Most people have
three, all abandoned. The problem is that every to-do app assumes the hard part is
*remembering* the tasks. For the ADHD brain, remembering was never the hard part.

The hard part is **starting.** The hard part is **choosing what's next.** The hard part is
**not drowning** in everything at once. The hard part is **feeling rewarded** enough to come
back tomorrow.

I GET IT DONE is the only app built end-to-end around those hard parts — from the
ten-second voice dump that catches a fleeting thought, to the four lanes that make
prioritizing a single tap, to the focus mode that shows one thing, to the dopamine loop
that makes finishing *feel* like winning.

That full loop — **capture → sort → start → finish → feel the win → come back** — is what
no other single tool offers. And for the people we serve, that loop is not a nice-to-have.
It is the difference between a day where they felt capable and a day where they felt like
they failed again.

## Content and Messaging Principles

Everything we produce — every video, every post, every ad — should feel:

**Understanding. Practical. Real. Validating. Encouraging.**

Not clinical. Not corporate. Not "hustle culture." Not shaming.

The tone is always: *"We get how your brain works, and here's something that actually
helps"* — never *"Here's why you should try harder."*

Specific guardrails:

- **Speak to the feeling, not the feature.** Lead with "the moment you sit down and freeze,"
  not "our 4-lane Kanban system."
- **Never shame.** Our audience has been told their whole lives they're lazy or disorganized.
  We are the opposite voice. We assume they're trying hard with a brain that works
  differently.
- **Show the relief, not the productivity.** The payoff isn't "do more." It's "finally feel
  on top of it." Sell the exhale.
- **Be a real person.** Talk like a friend who *also* has ADHD, not a software company.
- **Give value first.** Teach a real ADHD strategy (body doubling, time-boxing, brain
  dumping) in the content itself — then show how the app makes it effortless. Value first,
  product second.
- **No emojis, no clutter.** Our brand is calm and premium — Apple meets ESPN. The
  marketing should feel as uncluttered as the app. (This mirrors the product itself: clean,
  bold, distraction-free.)

This is how we build trust. And trust is how we build a community of people who don't just
use I GET IT DONE — they tell every ADHD friend they have, because for once, something
finally worked.
