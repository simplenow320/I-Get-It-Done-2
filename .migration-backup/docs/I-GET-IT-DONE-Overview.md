If you've ever stared at a to-do list and felt paralyzed instead of productive, this app was built for you.

## Why Most Task Apps Don't Work for Busy Minds

Most productivity apps assume you can sit down, organize your thoughts, and follow a neat list from top to bottom. But that's not how a busy brain works.

Research on motivation and attention shows that people who struggle with focus and follow-through aren't lazy. Their brains process reward signals differently. The dopamine pathways that help most people feel satisfied after checking something off? They don't fire the same way for everyone.

That means effort can feel less rewarding, small tasks can feel massive, and getting started can feel like pushing through a wall -- even when you actually want to do the thing.

I GET IT DONE is designed around this reality. Every feature exists because the science says it should help.

---

## The Science Behind the Features

Research identifies four things that drive motivation in busy, easily-distracted minds:

1. **Interest** -- Is this engaging?
2. **Urgency** -- Is there a deadline?
3. **Challenge** -- Is there something to beat?
4. **Novelty** -- Is this new or different?

When a task has none of these, your brain checks out. Our features are designed to add one or more of these to every task, every time.

## Core Features (Free Forever)

### The 4-Lane System

Instead of one long, overwhelming list, your tasks live in four simple lanes:

- **Now** -- What needs your attention today
- **Soon** -- Next few days
- **Later** -- Future weeks
- **Park** -- Ideas you're not ready to act on yet

**Why it works:** A single long list triggers overwhelm. Sorting into lanes reduces the number of decisions you have to make at any given moment. You only look at what matters right now. Everything else is safely stored but out of your mental space.

Overdue tasks automatically shift between lanes so nothing slips through the cracks without you having to manually reorganize.

### Quick Dump

Rapid-fire task entry. No organizing, no categorizing, no thinking about where it goes. Just get it out of your head.

There's even an "Unsure" option if you don't know which lane a task belongs in yet. Sort it later.

**Why it works:** The biggest barrier for busy minds is the startup cost of capturing a thought. If you have to make three decisions before you can write something down, you won't write it down. Quick Dump removes every barrier between "I need to do something" and "it's captured."

### Break It Down

Any task can be split into smaller subtasks. Each one gets a visual progress bar so you can see how far you've come.

**Why it works:** Research shows that a task with five steps can feel like a thousand steps when your brain isn't engaged. Breaking it into visible pieces makes each step feel doable. And watching the progress bar fill up creates small moments of accomplishment that keep you moving.

## Pro Features

### Voice Capture -- "Say It. Done."

Tap the microphone, say what's on your mind, and AI turns your words into organized tasks. No typing. No editing. Just talk.

**Why it works:** Busy minds often think faster than they can type. Speaking is the most natural way to capture a thought before it disappears. The AI handles the cleanup -- it strips the rambling and lays out clear action items.

This is the app's main advantage: your voice goes in, organized tasks come out.

### Focus Mode and Sprint Timer

A dedicated screen that shows you one task at a time. No distractions, no list of other things competing for your attention. Just the task in front of you.

Pair it with the sprint timer -- choose 10, 15, or 25 minutes -- and work against the clock.

**Why it works:** This directly applies two proven strategies from attention research.

First, the timer creates artificial urgency. Busy brains perform better under time pressure because it activates the part of the brain that says "this matters right now." But unlike waiting until the real last minute (which causes anxiety), the timer gives you controlled pressure with no consequences if you don't finish.

Second, the time limits prevent a task from feeling endless. Knowing "I only have to do this for 15 minutes" makes starting dramatically easier. The 25-minute option follows the Pomodoro technique -- a well-known method of working in short bursts with breaks in between. The 10 and 15-minute options exist because not everyone can do 25 minutes, and a shorter sprint you actually start is better than a longer one you avoid.

### Gamification -- Streaks, XP, Levels, and Achievements

Every completed task earns you points. Build streaks by staying consistent. Level up. Get achievement notifications when you hit milestones.

**Why it works:** When your brain's reward system doesn't naturally give you a satisfying feeling after finishing a task, you need to build that reward externally. Points, streaks, and level-ups create small dopamine hits that reinforce the behavior your brain struggles to reward on its own.

Streaks add daily urgency -- you don't want to break the chain. Achievements add challenge -- "can I hit the next milestone?" Both are proven motivators for minds that need something more than just a checked box.

### Team Delegation -- Hand Off Tasks

A Team Hub where you can invite others, delegate tasks, and track progress. Your teammate sees what you've assigned them, updates the status (Assigned, In Progress, Waiting, Needs Review, Done), and can add notes.

Your personal lanes stay completely private. Teammates only see what you explicitly hand off to them.

**Why it works:** Research highlights "body doubling" -- working alongside someone else -- as a powerful tool for focus and accountability. While this isn't the same as sitting next to someone, knowing that another person can see the task and is tracking it creates external accountability.

For people whose internal motivation system is unreliable, external accountability (someone else is watching) can be the difference between doing and not doing.

### Weekly Reset

A structured end-of-week review that shows you what you completed, what moved between lanes, what needs cleanup, and what to plan for next week.

**Why it works:** Busy minds often lose track of what they've actually accomplished. The weekly reset provides a moment of reflection that serves two purposes: it shows you concrete proof of your progress (which reinforces motivation), and it prevents task buildup by flagging stale items before they become overwhelming.

## How It All Fits Together

Here's the typical flow for someone using I GET IT DONE:

1. **A thought hits you** -- Open the app, hit Quick Dump or tap the mic. Get it out of your head in seconds.
2. **Sort when you're ready** -- Your tasks land in lanes. Move them around when it makes sense, or let the app auto-sort overdue items.
3. **Focus when it's time** -- Pick a task, enter Focus Mode, set a timer, and work. One thing at a time.
4. **Feel the progress** -- Watch subtask bars fill up. Earn XP. Keep your streak alive. Get a notification when you hit a milestone.
5. **Hand off what you can** -- Delegate tasks to your team. Track their progress without micromanaging.
6. **Review your week** -- See what you did. Clean up what's stale. Plan what's next.

No guilt. No overwhelm. Just forward motion.
